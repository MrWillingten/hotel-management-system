1. Introduction

1.1 Why this project

I chose the third project, the Hotel Reservation and Management System, over the two other options because it involves many calculations and a real degree of mathematical complexity, which I wanted to get the hang of and apply in a fully functional application. In a hotel, the price of a stay is never a single number. It depends on the number of nights and on the rate of each of those nights, which can change with the season or at the weekend; on the number of guests; on the extra services they use, such as breakfasts or an airport transfer; on any discount granted; on taxes that apply to some guests but not to others; on the deposit already paid; and, when a booking is cancelled at the last minute, on a cancellation fee.

Each of these amounts must be exact to the millime, and all of them must agree with one another on the final bill. A small mistake, such as counting one night too many or applying a tax twice, leads directly to a wrong invoice for a real customer. This combination of calculations that have to be correct and rules that have to be respected at every step is what made this project the most demanding of the three, and therefore the most interesting to me.

1.2 Aim of the project

My main aim is to show what a hotel management system can look like as a web application, equipped with the functions and features that a hotel needs in its daily work.

For the receptionist, the application should help to keep registration data consistent: each guest is recorded only once, with an identity document, a phone number and an e-mail address that have been checked. It should show the upcoming arrivals and departures of the day, and it should support tasks that would be very hard to carry out reliably without it, such as finding which rooms are free on given dates, preventing a room from being booked twice for the same night, and producing a correct bill when the guest leaves.

For the manager, the application should give full access to alter, change and modify the hotel's data and settings – rooms, prices, extra services, taxes and staff accounts – in order to maintain the system, while keeping up with the live status of the house and its incoming figures, such as occupancy and revenue, and with the other administration settings.

This document covers Checkpoint 1 (Analysis). It presents the requirements of the system (section 2), the business rules it must enforce (section 3), the entity–relationship diagram of its data (section 4) and the allocation of the tasks over the semester (section 5).

2. Project requirements

2.1 Problem statement

A single hotel in Tunisia manages its reservations with spreadsheets, phone calls and paper. This causes double bookings, wrong bills and no clear view of occupancy or revenue. The system replaces these tools with one application used by the hotel staff. It keeps the rooms and the guests, books rooms for date ranges without conflicts, handles changes and cancellations, records arrivals and departures, adds extra services to a stay, produces an itemised bill with taxes, payments and balance, and gives the management statistics on occupancy and revenue. Data must survive a restart, and every invalid operation must be refused with a clear explanation.

3. Business rules

Rules BR1–BR12 come from the project brief. Rules BR13–BR26 are my own design decisions, taken where the brief was incomplete or ambiguous.

3.1 Rules from the brief

#Rule
BR1Each room has a unique room number.
BR2Guests are identifiable by their registration information.
BR3A room cannot have overlapping confirmed reservations.
BR4The departure date is later than the arrival date (at least one night).
BR5A room under maintenance cannot be reserved for the affected period.
BR6A reservation cannot be confirmed without an available room.
BR7The number of occupants cannot exceed the room's capacity.
BR8Accommodation cost = number of nights × applicable nightly rates.
BR9Additional services are included in the final bill.
BR10A guest may pay a deposit before arrival.
BR11A 20 % cancellation fee applies when a confirmed reservation is cancelled less than 48 hours before arrival.
BR12Invalid operations are rejected and clearly explained.

3.2 Derived rules (design decisions)

#Rule
BR13A stay is a half-open interval [arrival, departure): two stays overlap only if arrival₁ < departure₂ and arrival₂ < departure₁, so a guest may arrive on the day another one leaves.
BR14Number of nights = departure − arrival, in days.
BR15Every active reservation (confirmed or checked in) blocks its rooms.
BR16Life cycle: Confirmed → Checked in → Checked out; Confirmed → Cancelled; Confirmed → No-show. Any other change is refused.
BR17Check-in is possible only for a confirmed reservation, on a valid date, and never while the previous guest of the room is still checked in.
BR18A modification runs every availability check again, ignoring the reservation itself.
BR19Extra services can be charged only while the guest is checked in, dated within the stay.
BR20Prices are locked at booking time, night by night: a later price change never alters an existing reservation or invoice.
BR21Payments and prices are positive; balance = total − paid; a negative balance means a refund is owed.
BR22An issued invoice can never be changed; corrections are new payments or refunds.
BR23The status of a room on a date is derived, by priority: unavailable (maintenance) > occupied > reserved > available.
BR24Money is in Tunisian dinars with three decimals and is stored as a whole number of millimes, never as a floating-point number.
BR25The receptionist searches for an existing guest before registering a new one; probable duplicates are flagged.
BR26A record that has a history (guest, room, service, tax, staff account) is archived, not deleted: hidden but kept and restorable. A record that was never used may be deleted.

3.3 Decisions on ambiguous points of the brief

QuestionDecision
Several rooms per reservation?Yes: one reservation may hold several rooms, all with the same dates; adults and children are recorded per room.
Cancellation fee20 % of the accommodation (after discount, before tax). Exactly 48 hours before arrival means no fee. The reference time is the arrival date at the check-in time (14:00 by default). The fee is taken from the deposit first.
DiscountsGranted by a manager only, as a percentage or a fixed amount, with a reason; the total is capped (30 % by default); on accommodation only.
Guest identityThe document type (CIN or passport) and its number are unique; phone and e-mail are contact details.
Early departure / no-showEarly departure: only the nights actually stayed are charged. No-show: recorded after the arrival time and charged like a late cancellation.
TaxesIn scope and configurable: a percentage (e.g. VAT) or an amount per adult per night (e.g. tourist tax). Each rule says who pays: everyone, foreign guests only or nationals only. A manager may exempt one stay from one tax, with a reason.
Occupancy rateRoom-nights sold ÷ room-nights available, rooms under maintenance excluded.

4. Entity–relationship diagram

Notation (crow's foot): ‖ exactly oneo‖ zero or oneo< zero or many‖< one or many PK primary key · FK foreign key · UQ unique · UQ¹ unique together

4.1 Reading the diagram

  • RESERVATION is the centre of the model: a guest makes reservations, and every charge, payment, discount, tax exemption and the final invoice belong to one reservation.
  • RESERVATION_ROOM resolves the many-to-many relationship between reservations and rooms: one reservation may include several rooms, and a room is booked in many reservations over time. It also records the adults and children in each room.
  • ROOM_NIGHT holds one row per room and per night, with the primary key (room_id, night). Because a primary key cannot appear twice, the database itself makes double booking impossible (BR3, BR5): a night is taken either by a reservation or by a maintenance period, never by both. Each row also stores the price locked at booking (BR20).
  • INVOICE and INVOICE_LINE are a frozen copy of the bill: they copy amounts and tax values instead of referring to the current prices, so an issued invoice never changes (BR22). A reservation has at most one invoice.
  • TAX_EXEMPTION links a reservation and a tax rule when a manager exempts one stay from one tax, with the reason.
  • All amounts are stored as whole numbers of millimes (BR24).

For readability, the diagram leaves out the technical tables used for security and operation: staff sessions, sign-in attempts, two-factor recovery codes, API tokens, one-time form tokens, the new-account walk-through, settings, invoice number counters and the daily figures frozen by the night audit. They are documented with the full relational schema in docs/data-model.md of the repository.
